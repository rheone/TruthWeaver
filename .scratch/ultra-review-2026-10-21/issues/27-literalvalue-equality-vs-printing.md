## Finding F1.1: LiteralValue equality ignores DateTimeOffset offset and decimal scale, but the printers keep both

Location: `src/TruthWeaver.Abstractions/LiteralValue.cs:313` (PLAUSIBLE, medium)

Equals and GetHashCode compare decimals with == (1.0m == 1.00m) and DateTimeOffsets with == (instant only, offset ignored). ToString (line 349, 351), and so TermIdentity.ToString, CanonicalPrinter (CanonicalPrinter.cs:177) and TreeFormatEmitter, keep the scale and the offset. Two terms are one TermIdentity (one memo key, one analyzer variable) while their canonical text and the PredicateArguments the predicate reads are different. CONTEXT.md 'Term identity' says arguments compare by 'exact type-normalized value', and ADR-0003 says canonical printed forms can be compared directly.

### Failure scenario

Take `p(at: "2024-01-01T00:00:00Z") AND p(at: "2024-01-01T02:00:00+02:00")`, where p is a custom predicate that reads args.GetDateTimeOffset("at").Offset or .Date (the calendar day in the written offset). The evaluator memoizes by TermIdentity, so p runs once and the second term silently reuses the first term's answer. A related case: `q(x: 1.0)` and `q(x: 1.00)` compile to equal Expression trees but give different CanonicalText, so a diff of canonical text reports a change where tree equality reports none.

### Expected behavior

Identity equality and canonical text agree. Either normalize at construction (decimal scale, a fixed offset or UTC) or include the scale and offset in Equals and GetHashCode.

## Comments

> *This was generated by AI during triage.*

Decision: normalize at construction (fix the decimal scale and the `DateTimeOffset` offset to a canonical form when the `LiteralValue` is built), not by widening `Equals`/`GetHashCode`. This keeps term-identity and memoization behavior unchanged (lower risk) at the cost of the author's exact written representation (e.g. a written `+02:00` offset becomes its UTC equivalent) — the safer of the two directions.

## Agent Brief

**Category:** bug
**Summary:** `LiteralValue.Equals`/`GetHashCode` treat `decimal(1.0)` and `decimal(1.00)` as equal, and two `DateTimeOffset`s at the same instant but different offsets as equal, but the scale/offset survives into the printed/canonical form — so two terms that are one `TermIdentity` (one memo key) can print as different text, and a diff of canonical text can report a change where tree equality reports none.

**Current behavior:** Decimal and `DateTimeOffset` literal equality compares by numeric value / instant only, ignoring scale and offset. `ToString()`, `CanonicalPrinter`, and the tree-format emitter all keep the original scale and offset. A predicate reading the exact written value (its offset, or its decimal scale) could observe different `PredicateArguments` for what the engine treats as one identical, memoized term.

**Desired behavior:** A `LiteralValue`'s scale/offset is normalized to a canonical form at construction (decimal to a fixed scale; `DateTimeOffset` to a fixed offset such as UTC), so that two literals which compare equal also print identically — equality and canonical text never disagree about whether two terms are "the same."

**Key interfaces:**
- `LiteralValue.OfDecimal`/`OfDateTimeOffset` (or wherever these are constructed) — normalize the input to the canonical scale/offset at construction time, rather than preserving the caller's original representation.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] `LiteralValue.OfDecimal(1.0m)` and `OfDecimal(1.00m)` are `Equals`-equal and print identically.
- [ ] `LiteralValue.OfDateTimeOffset` for the same instant at two different written offsets are `Equals`-equal and print identically.
- [ ] `TermIdentity`/canonical-text comparisons (redundancy analysis, coverage pairing, memoization) no longer disagree about whether two literals are "the same."
- [ ] The validation list in `CLAUDE.md` passes.

**Out of scope:**
- Widening `Equals`/`GetHashCode` to include scale/offset — considered and explicitly not chosen.

### Resolution

`LiteralValue.OfDecimal` drops trailing zeros and `OfDateTimeOffset` stores the instant at a UTC offset, so equal values print the same. The first plan (keep one fraction digit) was dropped: a whole decimal then printed as `1.0` and no longer matched the Int64 `1` in Tool.Core stub keys. Visible effects: a predicate reads a UTC offset, never the written one; a data-source decimal `18.0` reads as `18`. Docs: `CONTEXT.md` term identity. Tests: `LiteralValueTests`, `LiteralIdentityTests`; expectations updated in `CanonicalPrinterTests`, `DecisionRecordTests` and `JsonDataSourceTests`.
