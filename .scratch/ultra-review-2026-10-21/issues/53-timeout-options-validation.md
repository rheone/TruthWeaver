Location: `src/TruthWeaver/Evaluation/CompiledRule.Evaluation.cs:55` (PLAUSIBLE, low)

FaultBudget below 1 is rejected up front. A negative Timeout (other than -1 ms) or one above int.MaxValue ms only fails inside CancelAfter, with an undocumented ArgumentOutOfRangeException. In EvaluateManyAsync that happens during enumeration, not at the call as the docs promise for bad options. MaxElementEvaluations <= 0 is accepted, and every quantifier then faults. The third part — timeout being cooperative-only against a synchronous, non-token-observing predicate — is an inherent property of `CancellationToken`-based cancellation in .NET, not fixable without unsafe thread-abort patterns; document it rather than attempt to fix it.

## Agent Brief

**Category:** bug
**Summary:** `Timeout` and `MaxElementEvaluations` aren't validated upfront the way `FaultBudget` already is, so a bad value surfaces as a late, undocumented exception or a silently-wrong constant answer instead of an immediate, documented validation error.

**Current behavior:** `FaultBudget` below 1 is rejected immediately when options are constructed/applied. A negative `Timeout` (other than the documented infinite sentinel) or one exceeding the representable range only fails deep inside the cancellation machinery, with an undocumented `ArgumentOutOfRangeException` — and in `EvaluateManyAsync`, that failure surfaces during enumeration rather than at the call, unlike the documented contract for bad options. `MaxElementEvaluations <= 0` is accepted silently, and every quantifier then faults (a less useful failure mode than an upfront rejection).

**Desired behavior:** `Timeout` and `MaxElementEvaluations` are validated at the same point and with the same immediacy as `FaultBudget` — a negative `Timeout` (other than the infinite sentinel) or a non-positive `MaxElementEvaluations` is a documented, immediate validation error, not a late surprise. Separately, the documentation for `Timeout` states plainly that it's cooperative (bounded by the next cancellation-token check), not a hard wall-clock preemption — a predicate that blocks synchronously and never observes the token can still run past the configured timeout, and that's an inherent property of the mechanism, not a bug to fix here.

**Key interfaces:**
- Wherever `FaultBudget`'s upfront validation already happens — `Timeout` and `MaxElementEvaluations` need the same validation, at the same point.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [ ] A negative `Timeout` (other than the infinite sentinel) is rejected immediately with a documented exception, not deferred into `EvaluateManyAsync`'s enumeration.
- [ ] A non-positive `MaxElementEvaluations` is rejected immediately, instead of silently causing every quantifier to fault.
- [ ] Documentation states that `Timeout` is cooperative, bounded by the next cancellation check, and gives no hard preemption guarantee against a blocking, non-observing predicate.
- [ ] The validation list in `CLAUDE.md` passes.

**Out of scope:**
- Any attempt to forcibly preempt a blocking predicate that ignores its cancellation token — not fixable safely in .NET and explicitly not attempted here.
