## Finding L5.4: "could not be evaluated" (faulted) and "not evaluated" (skipped) are near-identical words for different states

Location: `src/TruthWeaver/Evaluation/DecisionExplainer.cs:77` (PLAUSIBLE, low)

`Explain` phrases a faulted term as "could not be evaluated" (DecisionExplainer.cs:75-78; TraceReasons.cs:65 says the same), while the trace, the decision record and the debugger views use "not evaluated" / `notEvaluated` for a term that short-circuiting skipped (TraceEntry.cs:24, TraceNode.cs:36, TraceReasons.cs:7, docs/decision-records.md:173). ADR-0002 and the glossary define NotEvaluated as skipped only. A faulted term is `notEvaluated: false` in the same record.

### Failure scenario

Rule `isOff AND boom` evaluated in ShortCircuit mode: the record shows `boom` with `notEvaluated: true` (skipped). The same rule in Exhaustive mode, with `boom` throwing: `Explain` returns "Not satisfied." for the False result but for `boom OR isOff` returns "Unknown because boom could not be evaluated" while the record shows `notEvaluated: false`. A support reader searching logs for "not evaluated" conflates skipped and faulted terms.

### Expected behavior

One word per state: faulted terms say "faulted" (the project term in CONTEXT.md), and "not evaluated" is reserved for short-circuit skips.

## Agent Brief

**Category:** bug
**Summary:** `DecisionExplainer`'s human-readable explanation phrases a faulted term as "could not be evaluated," nearly identical to the wording used elsewhere for a term that was skipped by short-circuiting ("not evaluated") — even though the two are distinct, differently-flagged states, so a reader searching explanation/log text for one conflates it with the other.

**Current behavior:** A faulted term's explanation reads "could not be evaluated." A skipped (short-circuited) term's trace/record wording reads "not evaluated" / `notEvaluated`. The two phrases are near-identical despite representing different, distinctly-flagged states (a faulted term has `notEvaluated: false`; a skipped term has `notEvaluated: true`).

**Desired behavior:** Each state gets a distinct word: a faulted term's explanation uses the project's own glossary term, "faulted," and "not evaluated" is reserved for short-circuit skips only.

**Key interfaces:**
- `DecisionExplainer`'s phrasing for a faulted term.
- `TraceReasons`'s equivalent phrasing.

**Acceptance criteria:**
- [x] A faulted term's explanation says "faulted" (or clearly distinct wording), not "could not be evaluated".
- [x] A skipped term's wording ("not evaluated") is unchanged.
- [x] The two states are no longer distinguishable only by reading the record's `notEvaluated` flag — the words themselves now differ.

## Resolution (2026-11-01)

Reproduced. The ticket text has no explicit Decision section; the Agent Brief names "faulted" (or clearly distinct wording) and that was followed. `DecisionExplainer` now says `faulted` for a faulted term (`Unknown because Over Limit faulted.`), and `TraceReasons.ForLeaf` (tool trace pane) says `Unknown: the term faulted (<fault message>)`. The skipped wording (`TraceReasons.Skipped`, the `notEvaluated` field) is unchanged. Not touched: `AssignmentSession` "The rule could not be evaluated." (a whole rule that failed to compile, a different state), `CaseCoverage` "case(s) could not be evaluated", and the historical `CHANGELOG.md` line. Note for the owner: the old `ExplainTests` test asserted the sentence did NOT contain "faulted" (no recorded reason); it now asserts the opposite and that "evaluated" is absent, so the two states cannot share words. Tests (red first): `ExplainTests.Explain_FaultedTerm_SaysItFaulted_Test` (renamed), `AssignmentSessionTests.EvaluateAsync_MissingVariable_IsUnknownWithItsFault_Test`.
