## Agent Brief

**Category:** enhancement
**Summary:** Add a regression test proving the compiler's own `MaxDepthExceeded` diagnostic fires correctly when actually reached, independent of the parser's depth guard.

**Current behavior:**
When a compiled tree's depth exceeds the configured maximum during the build pass (not the parse pass), the compiler reports a `MaxDepthExceeded` diagnostic with a specific message, `expected`/`found` text, and the offending node's path, and substitutes a failure placeholder for that subtree rather than throwing. No test currently drives this specific check: the parser's own depth guard (added separately) catches ordinary deep nesting before it reaches the compiler, so a test must reach the compiler's check by another path — most durably, a tree deep enough after definition expansion to exceed the depth limit post-expansion even though it looked shallow before expansion, or a tree constructed directly against the compiler's input type.

**Desired behavior:**
A tree that trips the compiler's own depth check causes a `CompilationResult` carrying a `MaxDepthExceeded` diagnostic with the expected message, `expected`/`found` text, and correct node path, and the subtree compiles to the standard failure placeholder rather than throwing or crashing.

**Key interfaces:**
- The compiler's build step reports `MaxDepthExceeded` with `expected`/`found` text and a `path` identifying the offending node.
- `CompilationResult` carries the diagnostic; compilation does not throw.
